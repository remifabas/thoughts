Airbrushing Mechanics & Practical Tips

Understanding Consistency %

The slider percentage represents the proportion of paint in the total liquid volume:

  • 20–25% (1:3 Paint:Thinner): Water-thin "Glaze" texture. Great for smooth filters and tinting, but prone to spidering if sprayed close.
  • 35–40% (1:1.5 Paint:Thinner): "Skim Milk" consistency. Ideal baseline for basecoating and priming with a 0.4mm needle.
  • 50% (1:1 Paint:Thinner): "Whole Milk" texture. Heavy coverage layer. Requires slightly higher pressure.

Why Limits Are Set at 10% and 90%

Practical physical limitations determine the slider bounds:

  • Under 10% (Too Thin): The acrylic binder breaks down completely. Paint cannot bind to resin/plastic and behaves like pure water, instantly pooling or running.
  • Over 90% (Too Thick): Unthinned paint straight from the bottle cannot clear the 0.4mm nozzle, leading to instant tip-dry and severe spitting/clogging.

Managing Spray Risks

  • Spidering (Blowout): Occurs when high air pressure blasts runny paint onto a surface at close range. Fix: Lower pressure, back away, or add paint.
  • Spitting / Dry Spray: Occurs when air pressure cannot atomize thick paint, or when paint dries mid-air before landing. Fix: Thin paint, raise pressure, or move closer.
